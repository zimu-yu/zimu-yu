## About me 👋

I'm Zimu Yu. My research lies at the intersection of **machine learning and computational biology**, with a particular interest in **protein fitness landscapes, mutational epistasis, and protein engineering**. I am interested in understanding how mutations shape biological function, and in developing computational methods that can make this process more accurate, efficient, and interpretable.

My background is in statistics, and I have always been drawn to the mathematical side of research. I especially enjoy **probability, mathematical statistics, and mathematical modeling**, and I like to look for the structure behind a problem: what assumptions are really being made, where the uncertainty comes from, and whether a biological question can be expressed in a cleaner mathematical form. For me, modeling is not only about obtaining a good prediction, but also about understanding why the prediction is possible in the first place.

Besides protein modeling, I also work on representation learning for **single-cell and multi-omics data**, where I am similarly interested in extracting meaningful structure from complex biological systems.

I am currently looking for **PhD opportunities** in computational biology, machine learning, and related areas. I would be sincerely grateful for opportunities to discuss research, potential projects, or possible PhD positions with researchers whose interests overlap with mine. I am always eager to learn, contribute, and grow through collaboration.
